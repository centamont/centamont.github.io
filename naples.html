Pelican Bay, Park Shore and the old town, a coast of mid-rise towers and low streets on the Gulf: where new buildings rise, how Florida law shapes every sale, and why most of Naples stays low.

Market note Later Updated October 21, 2026 4-minute read

The market in brief

Naples comes later for us, after Los Angeles and Dallas, with Tampa on the same coast. It is a market of second and seasonal homes as much as primary ones: buyers from the Midwest and the Northeast, many spending the winter here, and a growing number who make Florida their residence. New buildings are few and mostly mid-rise, so each one competes on its view of the Gulf, its finish and its amenities.

When we open Naples, its market sheet will follow the winter season as closely as the year, since so many of its buyers arrive for it, and will set Pelican Bay, outside the city, apart from Park Shore inside it. Our South Florida page covers the rules that apply across Florida, and Tampa lies to the north on the same coast.

Where new buildings rise

Pelican Bay and Vanderbilt Beach

North of the city, in unincorporated Collier County, where many of the area’s tallest residential towers stand behind the beach and the mangroves of Clam Pass. The city’s 42-foot limit does not reach here; Collier County’s zoning, site by site, sets how tall a building can be.

Park Shore

Inside the city, a line of residential towers along the Gulf, with Venetian Bay and its shops behind them.

The old town

Fifth Avenue South, Third Street South and the streets toward the pier, where buildings stay low and new residences come as small buildings and single houses. On the commercial streets the charter’s 42 feet and three stories hold, so a new building here is often residences over shops, sold on its walk to the beach and to Fifth Avenue rather than on a view.3

Naples Bay

The bay side of the old town and Port Royal, where residences look over boats and the bay rather than the Gulf, many with their own docks. Port Royal, at the bay’s south end toward Gordon Pass, is single houses on deep-water canals; the condominium buildings stand at the bay’s north end, near US 41.

How a purchase works here

15days to cancel a developer’s contract without penalty

A new Naples condominium usually sells in two steps: a refundable reservation that holds a unit, then a purchase contract once the developer’s documents are filed. Under section 718.503 (opens in a new tab), the contract starts a 15-day window to cancel without penalty, and a deposit schedule that rises in steps before completion. Under section 718.202 (opens in a new tab), the first 10 percent stays in escrow until closing; anything above it may fund construction if the contract allows.

Our buyer guides cover each step: buying pre-construction in Florida and buying new construction in Florida, from warranties and reserves to closing costs and the homestead exemption.

What a developer works with here

  • Filing before contracts. Under section 718.502 (opens in a new tab), a developer may not offer purchase contracts until its documents are filed with the state. Reservations can come earlier, refundable on request.
  • Deposits that can build. Deposits above 10 percent may fund construction where the contract allows, which changes how much equity and debt a project needs. That is not possible in New York.1
Read the other four
  • Which side of the city line. Inside the City of Naples, the charter holds commercial districts to 42 feet and three stories, so a site’s jurisdiction and zoning decide how tall a building can be. Read both before the land price is set.3
  • Storms and the Gulf. Hurricane Ian’s surge flooded much of the old town in September 2022.4 Buyers ask about elevation, flood zone and insurance first. Show the building’s design for storms plainly in the offering.
  • Reserves from the first budget. A developer-controlled association cannot waive or cut reserves, and buildings three stories or taller need a structural integrity reserve study.1 Monthly fees are part of the price buyers compare.
  • Buyers from New York. Marketing a Florida offering to New York residents brings New York’s filing rules with it, as our New York page explains.

Costs and rules at a glance

Deed stamp tax
70 cents per $100 of the price. A seller’s cost by custom, often passed to the buyer in developer contracts2
State income tax
None
Right to cancel
15 days from signing and receiving the documents, renewed by any amendment that materially changes the offering against the buyer1
Deposits
First 10 percent in escrow until closing; above that, available for construction if the contract allows1
Warranty
Three years on the unit from completion of the building; roof, structure and building systems up to five years1
Homestead
Up to $50,000 off the assessed value of a primary residence for most property taxes, $25,000 for school taxes, with yearly increases held to 3 percent at most, for owners in residence on January 12

A launch, step by step

10%the first deposits, held in escrow as the rest arrive on schedule
Read the five steps
  1. Before filing. Pricing, unit mix, plans and amenities are set against the buildings a buyer will compare. Reservations may be taken once the escrow agreement is approved, refundable on request.
  2. Filing. The developer files its documents with the state. Purchase contracts follow, each with its 15-day window to cancel.
  3. Construction. Deposits arrive on schedule; the first 10 percent stays in escrow. Weekly reporting shows whether pace meets the lender’s threshold.
  4. Completion. Walk-throughs, the certificate of occupancy and closings, one residence at a time.
  5. Turnover. The developer hands the association to the owners, with an inspection report for each building three stories or taller.1

What we will do here

In Naples, a site’s jurisdiction can settle its height before the design does, and buyers who spend the winter here set one building’s view of the Gulf, finish and storm design against the next. When we open here, advice starts with the site and with those buyers. We open Naples after Los Angeles and Dallas, with Tampa.
