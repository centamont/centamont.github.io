Houston

Uptown, River Oaks and the Museum District, where Houston’s new residential towers stand apart from its downtown offices: where new buildings rise, how Texas sells a new condominium, and what a city without zoning means for a new building.

Market note Later Updated October 16, 2026 4-minute read

The market in brief

Houston comes later for us, after Los Angeles and Dallas, with Austin. Unlike most cities, its luxury residential towers stand mostly outside downtown, in Uptown near the Galleria, beside River Oaks and in the Museum District. Its buyers include energy and medical executives, families leaving a large house in Memorial or River Oaks for a tower, and buyers from abroad who work in the city’s energy industry.

Where new buildings rise

Uptown and the Galleria

Post Oak Boulevard and the streets around the Galleria, where many of the city’s newest luxury residential towers stand.

River Oaks

The city’s best-known residential neighborhood of large houses and gardens, with a few residential towers at its edges.

The Museum District

Museums, Hermann Park and Rice University close by, with residential towers near the Texas Medical Center.

Downtown

The office towers of the city’s center, with a growing number of residences near Discovery Green and Buffalo Bayou.

How a purchase works here

Texas does not review a condominium offering before it is sold. Instead the developer must give each buyer a condominium information statement, and under section 82.156 of the Texas Property Code (opens in a new tab) a buyer who signs before receiving it has five days after receiving it to cancel, with every payment refunded. The developer cannot require the buyer to close until the statement has been delivered.

Under section 82.158 (opens in a new tab), deposits paid to the developer are held in escrow in Texas, by a title company, an attorney, a broker or an insured institution, until closing, a buyer’s default or a refund.

Closing costs are lower than in our other markets: Texas has no transfer tax and no state income tax. Property taxes are set locally and carry more of the cost of owning, so a buyer who makes the home a primary residence should apply for the homestead exemption, which since voters approved it in November 2025 takes $140,000 off the value for school taxes.

Texas has no statute that sets warranty periods for a new home, so the builder’s written warranty and the contract carry the weight. Read both before signing.

Our buyer guides cover each step: buying pre-construction in Texas and buying new construction in Texas, from the information statement and escrow to property taxes and the homestead exemption.

What a developer works with here

  • No state filing before the first contract. The launch date is set by the documents, the developer and the lender, not by a state review.
  • Deposits that stay in escrow. Deposits wait in escrow until closing, so construction is funded by equity and debt, as in New York, and the lender’s pre-sale requirement is the first milestone.
  • The statement before the signature. Delivering the information statement before the contract is signed keeps a late cancellation window from opening on a sale already made.
  • A city without zoning. Houston has no zoning ordinance, so land use is shaped by recorded deed restrictions and the city’s development rules. Read a site’s deed restrictions early, along with its neighbors’.
  • Water and storms. Buyers here ask about flooding first. Know a site’s flood history and how the building is designed for storms, and say so plainly in the offering.
  • Buyers from New York. Marketing a Texas building to New York residents brings New York’s filing rules with it, as our New York page explains.

A launch, step by step

  1. Documents. The declaration, the information statement and the escrow agreement are prepared, and prices and lines are set against the buildings buyers compare.
  2. Launch. Purchase contracts are signed, each buyer receiving the information statement first.
  3. Construction. Deposits stay in escrow. Weekly reporting shows whether pace meets the lender’s threshold.
  4. Closings. Title passes to the buyer, with no transfer tax due.

What we will do here

In Houston, where the towers stand outside downtown, the first question is which district a building’s buyers already live in; the second is what its deed restrictions allow.
