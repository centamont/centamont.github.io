Tampa

Downtown and Water Street, Harbour Island and Bayshore Boulevard, at the head of Tampa Bay: where new buildings rise, how Florida law shapes every sale, and what the bay asks of a new building.

Market note Later Updated October 21, 2026 4-minute read

The market in brief

50acres of Water Street, on land that was largely parking lots and warehouses

Tampa comes later for us, after Los Angeles and Dallas, as our first market on Florida’s Gulf coast. Its new residential towers gather downtown, where the Water Street district has risen beside the Channel District on land that was largely parking lots and warehouses,4 and along the water at Harbour Island and Bayshore Boulevard. Its buyers include the region’s executives and business owners, families moving from the Northeast and the Midwest, and Florida buyers who want a city home on the water for less than the same view costs in Miami.

Where new buildings rise

Downtown and Water Street

The business district along the Hillsborough River and the new Water Street district east of it, where offices, hotels and residential towers share streets laid out for walking, joined to the river by the Riverwalk.

The Channel District and Harbour Island

Warehouses and newer towers beside the port, and across a short bridge, Harbour Island, a residential island of condominiums and townhouses facing downtown.

Bayshore Boulevard and Hyde Park

A boulevard that follows the bay for miles beside a continuous waterfront walk, with Hyde Park’s streets behind it, running north to the University of Tampa. Condominiums along the boulevard look straight over the water.

Westshore

The office district beside Tampa International Airport, with its shopping at International Plaza, and to the south, residences rising by the marinas on Old Tampa Bay.

How a purchase works here

15days to cancel a developer’s contract without penalty

A new Tampa condominium usually sells in two steps: a refundable reservation that holds a unit, then a purchase contract once the developer’s documents are filed. Under section 718.503 (opens in a new tab), the contract starts a 15-day window to cancel without penalty, and a deposit schedule that rises in steps before completion. Under section 718.202 (opens in a new tab), the first 10 percent stays in escrow until closing; anything above it may fund construction if the contract allows.

Our buyer guides cover each step: buying pre-construction in Florida and buying new construction in Florida, from warranties and reserves to closing costs and the homestead exemption.

What a developer works with here

13days between Helene and Milton in 2024
  • Storms and the bay. In September 2024 Helene’s surge flooded streets around Tampa Bay, and thirteen days later Milton came ashore to the south, at Siesta Key.3 Buyers ask about elevation, flood zone and insurance first. Show the building’s design for storms plainly in the offering.
  • Filing before contracts. Under section 718.502 (opens in a new tab), a developer may not offer purchase contracts until its documents are filed with the state. Reservations can come earlier, refundable on request.
Read the other three
  • Deposits that can build. Deposits above 10 percent may fund construction where the contract allows, which changes how much equity and debt a project needs. That is not possible in New York.1
  • Reserves from the first budget. A developer-controlled association cannot waive or cut reserves, and buildings three stories or taller need a structural integrity reserve study.1 Monthly fees are part of the price buyers compare.
  • Buyers from New York. Marketing a Florida offering to New York residents brings New York’s filing rules with it, as our New York page explains.

Costs and rules at a glance

Deed stamp tax
70 cents per $100 of the price. A seller’s cost by custom, often passed to the buyer in developer contracts2
State income tax
None
Right to cancel
15 days from signing and receiving the documents, renewed by any amendment that materially changes the offering against the buyer1
Deposits
First 10 percent in escrow until closing; above that, available for construction if the contract allows1
Warranty
Three years on the unit from completion of the building; roof, structure and building systems up to five years1
Homestead
Up to $50,000 off the assessed value of a primary residence for most property taxes, $25,000 for school taxes, with yearly increases held to 3 percent at most, for owners in residence on January 12

A launch, step by step

10%the first deposits, held in escrow as the rest arrive on schedule
Read the five steps
  1. Before filing. Pricing, unit mix, plans and amenities are set against the buildings a buyer will compare. Reservations may be taken once the escrow agreement is approved, refundable on request.
  2. Filing. The developer files its documents with the state. Purchase contracts follow, each with its 15-day window to cancel.
  3. Construction. Deposits arrive on schedule; the first 10 percent stays in escrow. Weekly reporting shows whether pace meets the lender’s threshold.
  4. Completion. Walk-throughs, the certificate of occupancy and closings, one residence at a time.
  5. Turnover. The developer hands the association to the owners, with an inspection report for each building three stories or taller.1
