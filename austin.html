Rainey Street, the Second Street District and the towers along Lady Bird Lake, beside the tallest building in Texas. Where new buildings rise, and how Texas sells a new condominium with fewer filings and lower closing costs than our other markets.

Market note Later Updated October 21, 2026 4-minute read

The market in brief

Austin comes later for us, after Los Angeles and Dallas, together with Houston. Its new condominiums rise along Lady Bird Lake, on Rainey Street and in the Second Street District. Beside Rainey Street, a tower of apartments, offices and a hotel became the tallest building in Texas when it reached its full height in 2025.1 Its buyers include people moving to Austin for work from other states, and Texans who want a second home close to the city’s music, food and lake.

Before we open Austin, we watch how quickly the newest towers on Rainey Street and along the lake find their buyers, and how many of those buyers come from out of state for work. Our Dallas page covers the rules that apply across Texas.

Where new buildings rise

Rainey Street

A former street of bungalows on the lake’s north shore, now lined with residential towers, with the city’s tallest close by.

The Second Street District

Shops and restaurants south of City Hall, with residential towers near the old Seaholm power plant and the public library.

Congress Avenue

The avenue that runs from the lake up to the State Capitol, where hotels and residences have joined the office towers.

Along the lake

The towers that face Lady Bird Lake and its hike-and-bike trail, where the water and the parkland on both banks keep the view south open.

How a purchase works here

5days to cancel if the information statement arrives after you sign

Texas does not review a condominium offering before it is sold. Instead the developer must give each buyer a condominium information statement, and under section 82.156 of the Texas Property Code (opens in a new tab) a buyer who signs before receiving it has five days after receiving it to cancel, with every payment refunded.2 The developer cannot require the buyer to close until the statement has been delivered.

Under section 82.158 (opens in a new tab), deposits paid to the developer are held in escrow in Texas, by a title company, an attorney, a broker or an insured institution, until closing, a buyer’s default or a refund.

Closing costs are lower than in our other markets: Texas has no transfer tax and no state income tax. Property taxes are set locally and carry more of the cost of owning, so a buyer who makes the home a primary residence should apply for the homestead exemption, which since voters approved it in November 2025 takes $140,000 off the value for school taxes.3

No Texas statute sets warranty periods for a new home, so the builder’s written warranty and the contract carry most of the weight. Read both before signing, and ask an attorney what the law adds to them.

Our buyer guides cover each step: buying pre-construction in Texas and buying new construction in Texas, from the information statement and escrow to property taxes and the homestead exemption.

What a developer works with here

  • No state filing before the first contract. The launch date is set by the documents, the developer and the lender, not by a state review.
  • Deposits that stay in escrow. Deposits wait in escrow until closing, so construction is funded by equity and debt, as in New York, and the lender’s pre-sale requirement is the first milestone.
Read the other three
  • The statement before the signature. Delivering the information statement before the contract is signed keeps a late cancellation window from opening on a sale already made.
  • Protected views of the Capitol. State law protects view corridors to the State Capitol, twenty-six since a 2025 amendment removed four near the university, and the city protects more of its own. They limit how tall a building may rise on some downtown sites.4 Check a site against them before planning the massing.
  • Buyers from New York. Marketing a Texas building to New York residents brings New York’s filing rules with it, as our New York page explains.

A launch, step by step

Read the four steps
  1. Documents. The declaration, the information statement and the escrow agreement are prepared, and prices and lines are set against the buildings buyers compare.
  2. Launch. Purchase contracts are signed, each buyer receiving the information statement first.
  3. Construction. Deposits stay in escrow. Weekly reporting shows whether pace meets the lender’s threshold.
  4. Closings. Title passes to the buyer, with no transfer tax due.

What we will do here

In Austin, the Capitol’s view corridors can cap a downtown tower before its massing is drawn, and many of its buyers arrive from other states for work.
